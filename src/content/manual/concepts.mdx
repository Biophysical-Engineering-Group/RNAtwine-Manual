## The twine model in brief

RNAtwine is a minimal elastic model of RNA with one site per nucleotide.
Its helices are calibrated to the measured mechanics of double-stranded RNA, and its crossovers and kissing loops take the geometry of deposited structures.
Every parameter is the same for every design, so the shape a design relaxes to comes from its helices and from the direction and flexibility of its connecting arms, not from parameters fitted to that design.

Each site sits at its nucleotide's center of mass: backbone steps join successive sites, rungs join the two sites of each base pair, and cross-diagonals join neighboring pairs, so that each duplex is a truss.
A helix term holds the rise and twist of every base-pair step, and an axis-bend term sets how easily stacked helices bend.
Helices exclude volume as chains of capsules, and one steric rule keeps nucleotides from overlapping.

A design drawn in pyFuRNAce has ideal helices, and its kissing loops only approximately meet, so the drawing is not yet a shape the molecule can take.
A relax minimizes the model's energy starting from that drawing, and the kissing loops close onto templates taken from deposited loops of the same type.
As the loops close they pull on their helices, so the strain spreads through the whole structure instead of staying where the drawing put the mismatch.

<Figure name="concepts-loop-closing" />

The relax finds a basin of the folding landscape and its energy.
Because the model does not weigh basins by entropy or follow the path between them, it does not say which of several near-equal states forms, or how often: that is set by how the molecule folds and assembles.
When a design can take several states of nearly equal energy, such as rings of different sizes, the two isomers of a clasp or the stacking states of a junction, the set of states is the result.
A relax stays in the basin it starts in, so each of these states is built and relaxed on its own.

## The About window

<Figure name="concepts-about" />

**About**, at the foot of the rail, describes what RNAtwine does and how its model is built, gives the credits, and lists the values that the model uses for every design:

- the measured mechanics of a duplex that the helices are calibrated to: the bending and twist persistence lengths, the stretch modulus and the twist–stretch coupling
- the rest length of a crossover and the radius of the helix capsules
- the steric distances: the closest approach of two sites and the distance below which two nucleotides clash
- the deposited structures that the kissing-loop templates are taken from
- kT at 310 K

The app does not offer these values for editing, but a setup or configuration file can change some of them.
About then lists them under **Parameters of this setup** with their standard values and the values in use, and **Back to the standard parameters** restores the standard set.

## Kissing loops and clasps

A kissing loop is two hairpin loops whose bases pair with each other.
pyFuRNAce declares each one as two halves, which pair either with each other, as a clasp or a lid that holds the tile's shape, or with the matching half on another copy, as a connector.
Because the export declares both kinds in the same way, every loop needs a role that says which it is ([Loop roles](#loop-roles)).

A clasp can close in either of two isomers, related by a half turn like clasped hands with either thumb on top.
Because a relax stays in the isomer it starts in, you choose the isomer of each clasp in [Build](../build/#clasp-isomers), and the relax keeps it.

## Loop roles

| role | meaning |
|---|---|
| **close** | the halves pair with each other on the tile, and the relax closes the loop |
| **open** | the loop is left free, with no closing restraint |
| **join** | a connector to another copy: free on a lone tile, joined between copies in an assembly |

A role decides only what acts between the two halves of a loop: whatever the role, each half keeps its own shape and its pose on its stem, and the stems of an open loop still repel other helices.
On a lone tile, **open** and **join** give the same model, but only a **join** loop is offered as a site for joining copies in the **Dimer, ring or chain** setup.

Each loop takes its role from the first of these sources that names it:

1. The roles of the run, set in Build, in a `[loops]` table of a configuration file, or with `--role` on the command line.
2. The roles saved with the design, in `<name>.loops.toml` beside its `.dat`.
3. The default rule, under which a loop drawn with its halves within 4.15 nm of each other closes, and a loop drawn farther apart is a join when the run's assembly joins it across copies.

A relax starts only when every loop has a role, so any loop that none of these names has to be given one.
The default distance comes from the designs: the widest loop that a drawn tile closes on itself, the clasp of the deposited barrel tile, is drawn 4.15 nm apart, and every other loop drawn closed sits at 0.5 to 0.7 nm.
A loop drawn farther apart than that could be either a connector or a loop meant to close, so its role comes from you and not from a distance.

## Assemblies and junction states

An assembly is several copies of a tile joined through their connector loops: a dimer, a ring, a chain, a strip, or a tube or sheet.
Each join pairs the forward half of a loop on one copy with the reverse half on the next copy, so that the two run antiparallel, as the design's own pairs do.
Every copy is cut from the design as drawn, except in a tube or sheet, which by default starts from one relaxed tile.

**Junction stacking states.** At a four-way junction, two of the four strand steps stack, so that their helices continue coaxially across a nick, and the other two cross over.
The drawing fixes which steps stack, but the junction can also stack the other way, pivoted, with every helix end still stacked, and a copy joined through a loop on one of its arms can favor this state, which the drawing never shows.
So that a dimer can reach it, every terminal four-way junction with a joined site on its arms is relaxed in each stacking state, a terminal junction being one whose moving arms are unbranched stem-loops.
The lowest-energy state among those that converged is kept, and every state is listed.
A pivot that would open a loop the drawing closes is not tried, since the design never folds that way.

**Starts.** Because a relax stays in the basin it starts in, an assembly relax searches the arrangements that the copies can take from several starts and keeps the lowest in energy.
A dimer joined to itself through one loop both ways starts with the two copies facing each other at three offsets.
A ring of three or more identical copies is relaxed from a flat belt at two radii and from a ring about the tile's smallest axis.
A chain whose joins repeat starts from a short chain that is relaxed first, whose bent middle copy is laid along the chain at seven turns, so that a chain takes 7 to 23 relaxes.

**Symmetric solves.** Rings and dimers of identical copies are solved with exact symmetry: each copy is copy 0 turned about one axis, so that every copy has the same shape.

## Runs and run files

A run is the result of one relax: the relaxed coordinates, the drawn coordinates it started from, the report, and the settings it was solved under, which are its parameters, its assembly and junction state, and its loop roles.
So that a run opened again reports the same measures, a run file (`.rtrun`) stores the run exactly: a text header with the settings and the report, followed by the coordinates as raw numbers.
Relaxes are deterministic: the same design, settings and version of RNAtwine give the same result on any number of threads.
